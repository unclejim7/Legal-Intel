Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark: The First Real Scorecard for Legal AI

Harvey published initial results this week for its Legal Agent Benchmark (LAB) — the most rigorous public attempt to date to measure what AI agents can actually do on legal work. The benchmark, launched May 6, includes over 1,200 tasks across 24 practice areas, graded against 75,000+ expert-written rubric criteria using an all-pass standard (no partial credit — mirrors how legal deliverables actually work).

The initial leaderboard results are clarifying: Claude Opus 4.7 leads at 7.1%, with Sonnet 4.6 at 5.4%, Opus 4.6 at 4.2%, GPT-5.5 at 2.1%, and Gemini 3.5 Flash at 0.8%. These are not demo numbers. These are scores on complex, long-horizon legal tasks under conditions that mirror actual law firm work assignment.

The practical signal: agents are not yet autonomous for complex legal tasks, but the trajectory mirrors what happened in software engineering — coding agents "basically didn't work before December 2024 and basically work since." Harvey's framing is that LAB is designed to create the same legibility signal for legal agents. For WC/PI practitioners evaluating AI investments, the benchmark gives you a framework to ask vendors: "What's your LAB score?" instead of accepting marketing claims at face value.

The GitHub repo is open to anyone building or testing agents: github.com/harveyai/harvey-labs

AI Physical Injury Claims: The Liability Triangle Forming in WC Courts
Ohio WC practitioners report increasing claims where an AI system contributed to the injury mechanism — robots in warehouse fulfillment centers, AI-controlled manufacturing machinery, autonomous material handling. Under current Ohio law, the employer remains the WC respondent; but the robot or AI system manufacturer is a third party not immunized by the WC exclusive remedy. Plaintiff WC attorneys who file only the comp claim may be leaving a parallel product liability action on the table. The duty to investigate the third-party manufacturer claim is emerging as a competence question.

◼ Editor's Dispatch — Quick Takes
Take 01 · The Sanctions Escalation Ladder The AI hallucination sanction pattern now has a documented escalation arc: warnings → $1K–$5K fines → $10K+ → $30K+ → calls for disbarment. The NJ WC carrier appeal sanction (March 2026) is notable not for its dollar amount but for its fact pattern — opposing counsel flagged the fake citations and the attorney failed to act. Courts are less forgiving of inaction than of initial error. WC practitioners operating in multi-state practices should implement a citation verification protocol now, before the first violation, not as a remediation response after one.
Take 02 · The 7.1% Problem and What It Actually Means for Practice Harvey's LAB result that Claude Opus 4.7 — the current best model on legal tasks — passes only 7.1% of complex legal tasks autonomously is widely being read as "AI isn't ready." That's the wrong read. The correct read is: AI agents are production-ready for some tasks and not others, and the benchmark tells you which. For WC/PI practitioners, tasks like medical chronology extraction, form completion, demand package structuring, and intake triage are far more amenable to current AI than complex causation analysis or appellate brief writing. Build your workflow around the capability gap, not against it.
Take 03 · Illinois HB 3773 Is a Plaintiff WC Attorney's Hidden Weapon Illinois practitioners are almost universally focused on HB 3773 as a compliance burden for employer-clients. That framing misses the offensive play. If a carrier or third-party administrator uses an AI system that routes modified duty assignments, voc-rehab referrals, or return-to-work decisions in a way that has disparate impact on a protected class — national origin, age, disability — that's an IHRA claim running alongside the WC petition. The IDHR's broad definition of "AI" catches vendor tools that employers deploy. Plaintiff WC practitioners in Illinois should be asking: what AI systems does the carrier use to manage this claimant's file?
