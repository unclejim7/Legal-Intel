◼ The Agentic Corner

The "Context Tax" Problem: Why Harvey Acquired DeepJudge to Solve It

Every time a legal AI agent starts a new task, it starts without memory of the prior 10,000 tasks your firm has run through the same platform. That's the "context tax" — the cost of re-establishing institutional knowledge at the start of every session. It's why an AI that drafts a brilliant contract clause today may draft a subtly inconsistent one tomorrow, because it has no access to what your firm has approved, argued, or rejected in past matters.

Harvey's acquisition of DeepJudge in early 2026 was explicitly targeted at this problem. DeepJudge had built a knowledge-graph architecture for institutional legal memory — a way to make a firm's prior work product, playbooks, and approved positions continuously available to AI agents without having to re-inject them into every new context window. Harvey's Command Center interface now surfaces this institutional knowledge layer alongside its 25,000+ custom agents, so an agent drafting a coverage position letter can access how your firm has argued that same coverage issue across 400 prior cases.

For WC/PI firms, the near-term practical version of this is simpler: the firms that are building structured case outcome databases now — documenting what worked, what didn't, what settlement ranges by injury type, which arbitrators respond to which framing — are building the raw material for institutional memory AI. The firms treating every closed case as a transaction to be filed and forgotten are not.

◼ Case Law & Regulatory Radar
Illinois — Appellate WC
Azcon Metals v. IWCC
2026 IL App (5th) 250301WC (Feb. 26, 2026)

The Fifth District affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded in this wage-differential credit dispute. The core issue: whether an employer is entitled to a credit for scheduled-loss payments (§8(e)) when a claimant subsequently recovers under §8(d)(1) wage differential. The court held that where a claimant with a line-of-work injury recovers under §8(d)(1), the employer is entitled to the credit. But under the IWCC's reading of §8(d)(2), that credit does not apply — an asymmetry the court found textually defensible but logically strained.

◼ Illinois Spotlight

HB 3773 Is Operative Now — The Postponed Hearing Changes Nothing About Current Exposure

The IDHR's June 10 hearing postponement has caused some employer-side practitioners to treat the Subpart J rulemaking as paused. It is not. HB 3773 has been in effect since January 1, 2026. The proposed regulations interpret and implement a statute that is already operative. An employer using AI to evaluate return-to-work fitness, score job applications from injured workers, or run claims-severity triage today has potential IHRA exposure regardless of when final rules issue.

Illinois WC attorneys representing injured workers should be asking two questions on every new matter: (1) Did the employer or carrier use AI in any decision affecting the claimant's employment terms, RTW assessment, or claim handling? (2) If so, did the claimant receive any written notice of that AI use? The absence of notice is, under HB 3773's plain text, a violation — and the proposed rules make clear that carriers and their vendors acting on an employer's behalf are within scope.

◼ Medical & Expert Intelligence

When AI Imaging Correlates, Not Causes: The Litigation Risk of Over-Relying on Enhanced IME Scans

AI-enhanced MRI and 3D reconstruction technology is expanding what IME physicians can detect in a single examination — including subtle disc degeneration, ligament micro-tears, and soft-tissue findings that previously required specialist radiologist consultation. But the litigation risk runs in both directions. Correlation is not causation, and a sophisticated AI imaging tool that identifies pre-existing degenerative changes may be used to argue that a claimant's current condition is unrelated to the workplace injury — even where the degenerative findings were clinically silent before the accident.

Plaintiff counsel should be preparing to challenge AI imaging conclusions on Daubert/Frye grounds, including requests for the underlying algorithm's training data, validation methodology, and accuracy benchmarks. Defense counsel and carriers using AI-assisted IME QA should be aware that the audit trail generated by those QA tools — including any flagged inconsistencies in the original report — may be discoverable.

The Illinois Workers' Compensation Commission resolves disputes between injured workers and employers, with cases first tried by an arbitrator, then reviewable by a three-commissioner panel, and then appealable to the circuit court, appellate court, and Illinois Supreme Court. The vast majority of disputes are resolved by settlement before reaching the appellate level.
📖 Word of the Day
Wage Differential — Under §8(d)(1) of the Illinois Workers' Compensation Act, a claimant whose earning capacity has been reduced due to a work-related injury may recover two-thirds of the difference between their pre-injury average weekly wage and the average weekly wage they are capable of earning post-injury. The wage-differential computation is among the most frequently litigated damages issues in Illinois WC practice.
