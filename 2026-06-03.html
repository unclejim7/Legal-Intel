Harvey's partnership with DeepJudge — announced at the Harvey Forum in New York on May 20 — puts a name to a problem that every WC/PI practitioner using AI has already encountered without knowing what to call it: the "context tax."

Here's how it works: even the most capable legal AI models produce generic output when they can't access a firm's prior work product, negotiating positions, settled cases, and institutional decision-making. The AI knows the law in the abstract. It doesn't know that your firm has a standing position on IME causation for lumbar claims at a particular defense carrier, or that a given adjuster settles soft-tissue cases at 2.8x specials in your jurisdiction. That gap — between generic legal AI and firm-specific institutional intelligence — is the context tax.

DeepJudge, built by former Google AI researchers with ETH Zurich doctorates and backed by Coatue and Felicis, ingests a firm's entire historical matter database, applies semantic understanding, and connects that knowledge to Harvey's drafting and research workflows in real time. Output generated by Harvey gets fed back into DeepJudge — each new matter strengthens the institutional memory rather than being discarded. The system respects existing access controls and ethical walls.

For WC/PI builders and practitioners: this architecture — AI drafting/research grounded in firm-specific prior work — is the design pattern to watch. Firms that start building structured repositories of prior demand packages, negotiated positions, and settlement outcomes today will have the institutional training data to power this model when it becomes available at their price point. Firms that don't will keep paying the context tax.

The firms that will get the most out of the next generation of legal AI (Harvey + DeepJudge, CoCounsel + Supio, whatever comes next) are the ones building structured, searchable repositories of that institutional knowledge today. ⚖️ Legal Fact
Illinois was the first state in the nation to enact a law regulating AI in job interviews — the Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (820 ILCS 42), effective January 1, 2020. It required employers using AI to analyze video interviews to notify applicants, explain how the AI works, obtain consent, and limit sharing of footage. Six years later, Illinois has now extended AI employment regulation to all covered employment decisions under HB 3773 — making it one of only three U.S. jurisdictions (alongside NYC and Colorado) with comprehensive employer AI-decision mandates.
📖 Word of the Day
Apportionment (n.) — In workers' compensation, the allocation of an injured worker's disability between a work-related cause and a pre-existing condition or non-occupational factor. In Illinois WC practice, apportionment disputes are among the most heavily contested issues at arbitration — employers argue that pre-existing degenerative conditions bear a share of permanent impairment, while claimants assert that the work injury "lit up" or materially aggravated the underlying condition. AI medical chronology tools are increasingly used to map pre-injury baseline health against post-injury treatment records, making apportionment arguments more data-driven — and more challengeable — on both sides.
