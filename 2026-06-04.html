◼ ABOVE THE FOLD

COLORADO KILLS ITS OWN AI LAW BEFORE IT TAKES EFFECT — AND ILLINOIS FILLS THE VACUUM

The nation's most-watched AI employment law died before it could live. Colorado Governor Jared Polis signed SB 26-189 on May 14, 2026 — a wholesale replacement of the state's landmark 2024 Colorado AI Act, which had been scheduled to take effect June 30. The original law would have imposed bias audits, full risk management programs, and algorithmic discrimination assessments on any employer deploying AI in hiring, promotion, or termination decisions. After sustained pushback from the tech industry, a constitutional challenge filed by xAI (joined by the DOJ), and a federal magistrate's April 27 stay, the Colorado legislature passed the rewrite in the final days of its session. The replacement law strips the substantive obligations down to transparency-only: employers must now merely disclose that AI is being used in covered decisions, with no mandate for bias testing or impact assessment. Illinois, meanwhile, is moving in the opposite direction: IDHR's Subpart J comment window on HB 3773 implementation rules closed June 29, and the June 10 public hearing was postponed June 2 for "continued collaboration with other state agencies" — a delay that signals substantive rule revision is underway, not retreat.

Reporter's Take: Colorado's retreat from substantive AI accountability creates a federal regulatory vacuum that Illinois is uniquely positioned to fill — and the state's BIPA enforcement history suggests it will. For WC/PI practitioners whose carrier and TPA clients use AI in claims triage, RTW evaluation, and disability management, the Illinois trajectory is the one to watch. The June 29 comment deadline is not an abstraction; it's the last opportunity to shape rules that will govern your clients' exposure.

HB 3773 OPERATIVE EXPOSURE ANALYSIS: WHAT ILLINOIS WC PRACTITIONERS MUST ASSESS NOW

Illinois HB 3773 / Public Act 103-0804 has been in effect since January 1, 2026. The law prohibits the use of AI that has the effect of discriminating against employees in covered employment decisions — including RTW determinations, discipline, and the "terms, privileges, or conditions of employment" — even if the discrimination is unintentional. It also prohibits using zip codes as proxy for protected classes.

For WC practitioners, the critical question is whether AI tools used by employers, carriers, and TPAs in RTW and accommodation decisions are covered. The IDHR's proposed Subpart J rules (pending final publication after the June 10 hearing postponement) define "make or influence covered employment decisions" broadly. A carrier-side AI tool that evaluates an injured worker's RTW fitness and feeds that evaluation to an employer's HR system is almost certainly in scope.

Violations expose employers to actual damages, civil penalties, attorneys' fees, and compliance reporting obligations — the same remedial framework as other IHRA civil rights violations. With comment window open through June 29, this is the moment for WC stakeholders to engage.

◼ MEDICAL & EXPERT INTELLIGENCE

AI-ENHANCED IME IMAGING: BOTH A CAUSATION TOOL AND A DAUBERT TARGET

AI-enhanced MRI imaging is now appearing in IME reports as a clinical adjunct — platforms that apply machine learning to standard imaging to surface findings not visible on conventional reads. For plaintiff counsel, this creates a new discovery obligation: when an IME physician uses an AI imaging tool to support a causation opinion, the platform, its training data, its validation studies, and its output in this specific case are potentially discoverable and Daubert-challengeable.

Defense counsel using these tools to bolster denial opinions face the same exposure in reverse. The evidentiary landscape is shifting: AI-enhanced imaging findings may be powerful, but they require the same foundational expert disclosure as any other methodology. "The IME physician used an AI imaging assistant" is not a complete disclosure.

Begin asking in IME depositions: which imaging AI platform was used, was it FDA-cleared for the claimed use, what is its published sensitivity and specificity, and what did it flag in this case that the standard read did not?

◼ EDITOR'S DISPATCH
01

Colorado's Retreat Is Illinois's Opening

When the most ambitious state AI employment law in the country collapses before its effective date, it creates a standard-setting vacuum. Illinois is the most likely state to fill it — not because of political coincidence but because of institutional capacity: BIPA enforcement infrastructure, a functioning IDHR rulemaking process, and a legislative history of moving first on technology employment regulation. WC practitioners whose carrier clients operate nationally need to calibrate their compliance exposure to Illinois, not Colorado.

02

The Hallucination Rate Is Not Improving Fast Enough

17% and 33% hallucination rates for the two dominant legal research platforms, cited by the Ninth Circuit in a published discipline order, means roughly 1 in 6 to 1 in 3 AI-generated legal research answers contain errors. That is not a marginal quality issue; it's a structural workflow risk. The answer is not to stop using AI research tools — it's to build verification into the workflow as a non-negotiable step, the same way you cite-check before filing. The attorneys sanctioned this week failed at that step. Don't.

03

The 56% Under $5K Problem

More than half of PI firms spend under $5,000 annually on AI. That's less than one associate's monthly salary, less than a single expert witness deposition, less than many court reporter bills. The LawPro.ai data doesn't suggest these firms aren't aware of AI — 92% say they're using it. They're using it at the lowest possible cost tier: generic tools, no integration, no workflow investment. The gap between that tier and firms building real operational AI capability is widening every quarter. The median is not safe.

⚖ LEGAL FACT OF THE DAY

The Illinois Workers' Compensation Act's permanent partial disability schedules assign specific numbers of weeks to each body part lost or impaired — the thumb is worth 76 weeks, the entire arm 253 weeks, and loss of hearing in one ear 54 weeks. These scheduled values are fixed regardless of the claimant's wage or occupation and cannot be combined with a wage differential award under Section 8(d)(1) per Azcon Metals v. IWCC.

📖 WORD OF THE DAY

Agentic AI (n.) — An AI system that autonomously performs multi-step tasks by planning, selecting tools, and taking sequential actions toward a defined goal, without requiring a human to direct each step. Distinguished from chatbots that respond to single prompts.
